University lecture courses

The watch or listen badge on every lecture entry points to one of these five courses. Full recordings, free, no login.

Justice

Michael Sandel, Professor of Government at Harvard. One of the most-watched university courses ever recorded, filmed live in the lecture hall.

Used on Power, Justice & State
Philosophy and the Science of Human Nature

Tamar Gendler, Professor of Philosophy and Psychology at Yale.

Used on Truth, Illusion & Deception (Ring of Gyges), Individual & Collective (Hobbes)
Death

Shelly Kagan, Professor of Philosophy at Yale. One of Yale's most popular electives.

Used on Mortality, Fate & Transcendence
European Civilization, 1648–1945

John Merriman, Professor of History at Yale.

Used on Hubris, Progress & Nature
Human Behavioral Biology

Robert Sapolsky, Professor of Biology and Neurology at Stanford.

Used on Cosmic Awe & Evolution (Behavioral Evolution), The Stranger, The Self & The Other (Aggression, III)

Podcasts & video analysis

The deeper badge on a work points to one of these.

History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps

Peter Adamson, Professor of Philosophy at LMU Munich and King's College London.

A complete history of philosophy, told chapter by chapter, with no gaps skipped.

Used on Truth, Illusion & Deception (Plato's Cave), Individual & Collective (Marcus Aurelius), Power, Justice & State (Machiavelli)
The ThinkND Podcast

Notre Dame's public scholarship platform, featuring the university's own faculty.

Brings academic research to a general audience outside the classroom.

Used on Truth, Illusion & Deception (Rear Window)
The Lonely Palette

Tamar Avishai, art historian, Tufts.

Breaks down one painting per episode for listeners with no art history background.

Used on Individual & Collective (Seurat, La Grande Jatte)
Bloomsbury Academic Podcast

Interviews with the authors of Bloomsbury's academic film-studies books.

Puts the film scholarship directly in the words of the person who researched it.

Used on Power, Justice & State (The Godfather)
The Great Books (National Review)

A different named literature scholar hosts each episode, drawn from schools like the University of Dallas and Hillsdale College.

Gives listeners a way into a long, canonical novel through discussion.

Used on Mortality, Fate & Transcendence, Hubris, Progress & Nature, The Stranger, The Self & The Other
Historic Royal Palaces Podcast

Run by the UK charity that manages Hampton Court Palace and the Tower of London.

Draws on the palaces' own archives and curators.

Used on Mortality, Fate & Transcendence (Holbein, The Ambassadors)
ArtCurious

Jennifer Dasal, curator at the North Carolina Museum of Art.

Digs into the stranger, scandalous, and overlooked stories behind art history.

Used on The Stranger, The Self & The Other (Manet, Olympia)
Painting of the Week

Phil Grabsky, the BAFTA-winning documentarian behind Exhibition on Screen.

A short, focused look at a single painting, one per episode.

Used on Cosmic Awe & Evolution (Friedrich, Wanderer above the Sea of Fog), Hubris, Progress & Nature (Turner, Rain, Steam and Speed)
NPR Fresh Air

NPR's long-running interview and culture program.

Places a work in its broader cultural and political context.

Used on Truth, Illusion & Deception (Daft Punk review), Power, Justice & State (Fela Kuti, Zombie)
Dissect

A single podcast season devoted to one album, track by track.

Treats an album with the same depth of analysis as a novel or a film.

Used on Truth, Illusion & Deception (Daft Punk, Random Access Memories), The Stranger, The Self & The Other (Kendrick Lamar, To Pimp a Butterfly)
Soul Music (BBC Radio 4)

A long-running BBC Radio 4 documentary series, produced by the BBC.

Traces the history and emotional impact of a single piece of music through the people it's affected.

Used on Cosmic Awe & Evolution (Bach, Cello Suite No. 1)
Sticky Notes: The Classical Music Podcast

Joshua Weilerstein, conductor, former assistant conductor of the New York Philharmonic, now music director of the Orchestre National de Lille.

Breaks down a single classical piece, movement by movement, for listeners without a music theory background.

Used on Mortality, Fate & Transcendence (Beethoven, Symphony No. 5), Hubris, Progress & Nature (Stravinsky, The Rite of Spring)
Quick take

Reviews & criticism

The review badge points here.

RogerEbert.com

Roger Ebert's own reviews, continued today by Matt Zoller Seitz, Brian Tallerico, and the rest of his successor critics.

Opinionated, plainly written film criticism for a general audience.

Used across every film entry, all seven themes
Smarthistory

Founded by Dr. Beth Harris and Dr. Steven Zucker, art historians, as a free, museum-backed alternative to a paywalled art history textbook.

In short written form on most art entries; embedded as video on works where they'd filmed one.

Used across most art entries, including Truth, Illusion & Deception (Magritte, video) and Power, Justice & State (David, The Death of Socrates, video)
Rolling Stone

One of the longest-running, most established music publications, with decades of album criticism behind it.

Used on Individual & Collective (Pink Floyd)
